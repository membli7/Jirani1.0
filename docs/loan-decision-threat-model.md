# Threat Model: Chama Loan Eligibility and Review

**Date:** 12 November 2026 · **Milestone:** Local implementation review; production deployment not verified · **Owner review:** Pending

## Feature description

A Chama configures its own loan eligibility policy. The server checks membership duration, recorded share-capital contributions, amount tiers, Chama balance, existing member principal, and two guarantors’ available share capital, then records an automatic approval or rejection. A member can request one post-decision human review; officers can inspect records. The App estimates a reducing-balance repayment schedule but does not disburse money or record the actual off-App disbursement.

## Data flow

Borrower browser → session-authenticated Express API `||` → server-side policy evaluator → Chama-scoped loan, transaction, and audit records in the configured data store. The estimate endpoint is an authenticated, read-only API request; it returns the caller’s quote with `Cache-Control: no-store` and is limited to 60 requests per minute per Chama member.

Officer browser → authenticated review API `||` → conflict and role checks → saved decision, reason, review response, and audit record. No credit bureau, bank, mobile-money provider, or external disbursement service is called by this workflow.

## STRIDE analysis

| # | Threat | Category | Likelihood | Impact | Risk | Mitigation and status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A member changes the request body, Chama ID, interest model, amount, or guarantor IDs to bypass policy or alter the repayment cost. | Tampering | Medium | High | **High** | The server derives the policy, Chama rate, balance, contribution history, existing principal, and guarantor capacity from authenticated group records; validates the amount, term, and two distinct same-Chama active guarantors; and rejects an attempted flat-model override. A loan snapshots its applied rate. Cross-Chama and tampering regression tests pass. **Implemented.** |
| 2 | A stolen or compromised member session is used to submit an application or read that member’s loan records. | Spoofing | Medium | High | **High** | Loan endpoints require an authenticated session and completed PIN-change flow; authorization is checked server-side, not trusted from the browser. Existing session and recovery controls apply. **Existing controls; production session configuration and account-recovery operation still need review.** |
| 3 | A member supplies another Chama’s identifiers or guesses a record to expose its rate, decision inputs, or financial history. | Information disclosure | Medium | High | **High** | Loan calculations and records are scoped to the authenticated Chama; the estimate contains only the caller’s submitted amount/term and that Chama’s rate. Tests show that a second Chama’s rate cannot affect the first. **Implemented; retain cross-tenant tests in CI.** |
| 4 | A non-authorized or conflicted officer changes eligibility policy or alters a pending human-review outcome. | Elevation of privilege | Medium | High | **High** | Policy updates require Chairperson authorization; review actions require an authorized non-conflicted officer; an officer vote is not exposed for new applications. **Implemented; keep server authorization tests mandatory.** |
| 5 | A member or officer disputes having made a request, decision, or review response, or a person with direct store access alters the evidence. | Repudiation | Medium | High | **High** | Decision and review records include actor, time, policy version, criteria/reasons, and outcome. The current audit store is not tamper-evident. **Partially mitigated.** Add an append-only or independently protected audit trail and retention/access controls before relying on it in a financial dispute. |
| 6 | An authenticated user repeatedly requests estimates to consume API or compute resources. | Denial of service | Medium | Medium | **Medium** | Inputs are bounded, the schedule has at most six installments, and the endpoint is throttled to 60 requests per minute per Chama member. The shared limiter uses Redis only where its production configuration enables it; add or verify edge-level limits and alerting for the actual deployment. **Implemented locally; deployment verification remains.** |
| 7 | Incorrect group data, poorly chosen thresholds, or an unexplained automatic rejection unfairly affects a member; a nominal review route may not provide meaningful intervention. | Tampering / Elevation of privilege | Medium | High | **High** | The decision records its rule version and inputs, the member sees the reasons and can request human review, and the reviewer can check source records. Before real lending, the controller must complete any required DPIA, test errors and disparate outcomes, verify member notices and lawful basis, monitor review requests, set a practical response target and escalation route, and obtain Kenyan legal advice on the actual entity and lending model. **Production gate; not established by local code tests.** |
| 8 | An automatically approved record is mistaken for a loan that was actually disbursed, or its projected schedule is treated as a binding debt despite a different real advance date or cost. | Repudiation / Tampering | Medium | High | **High** | The member form and terms say that approval does not transfer funds; the estimate and saved schedule show the rate and installments. The App does not verify, record, or timestamp an off-App disbursement. **Residual production risk.** Before real lending, add an authorized, auditable disbursement confirmation (amount, date, source reference, and actual lender) and make the debt schedule depend on that confirmed event. |

## Security stories generated

- [x] **Prevent client-controlled loan decisions** — P1 — As the system, I want eligibility, Chama rate, and repayment model derived and validated server-side so a modified request cannot bypass group rules. **Threat:** T1 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given altered Chama/model/guarantor fields, when a request is submitted, then the server rejects or recalculates it using the authenticated Chama’s policy and records the applied rate. **Completed in this change; integration tests pass.**
- [x] **Isolate loan data by Chama** — P1 — As the system, I want every loan query and quote scoped to the authenticated Chama so members cannot read another group’s rate or decision history. **Threat:** T3 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given two Chamas with different rates and balances, when either member requests a quote or loan list, then only that member’s Chama data is used. **Completed in this change; tests pass.**
- [x] **Restrict policy and review actions** — P1 — As the system, I want server-side role and conflict checks so only an authorized officer can configure rules or conduct a non-conflicted human review. **Threat:** T4 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given a member, borrower, guarantor, or unauthorized officer, when they try the restricted action, then the server denies it and records no change. **Existing/covered by current route checks; keep tests in CI.**
- [x] **Bound quote-resource use** — P2 — As the service operator, I want a per-member estimate limit and non-cacheable responses so abusive quote loops are constrained and stale group rates are not reused. **Threat:** T6 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given more than 60 estimate requests in one minute for one member, when the next request arrives, then it is rejected with 429 while another member’s allowance remains separate. **Implemented and regression-tested.**
- [ ] **Operate human review and automated-decision safeguards** — P1, before real lending — As the responsible controller, I want a completed DPIA where required, verified notices and lawful basis, data-quality/fairness checks, monitored review requests, response targets, and an escalation route so a member can obtain meaningful human intervention. **Threat:** T7 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given a member requests review, when the request is submitted, then a named responsible reviewer is notified, the request is tracked to a communicated deadline, the member can add their perspective, and the outcome/reasons are retained. **Owner and legal review required.**
- [ ] **Confirm actual loan disbursements** — P1, before real lending — As the Chama and lender, I want the actual amount/date and evidence of an off-App advance recorded before the repayment obligation is treated as funded so an approval cannot be confused with a transfer. **Threat:** T8 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given an approved application, when no disbursement is recorded, then the app does not represent the projected schedule as a funded debt; once the authorized lender confirms the advance, the app stores the amount, date, actor, and evidence reference and uses that event for the repayment schedule.
- [ ] **Strengthen audit integrity** — P2 — As the operator, I want loan decisions, rule changes, reviews, and disbursements preserved in an append-only or independently protected audit trail so a direct data-store edit is detectable. **Threat:** T5 · **Source:** this threat model. **Acceptance:** Given an administrator attempts to rewrite or remove a past loan event, when the audit record is verified, then the change is blocked or independently detected and attributable.

## Residual risks

- **Low:** The estimate returns a quote only for the authenticated member’s explicitly supplied amount and term; it does not return other members’ inputs or submit a loan application. This remains low impact while Chama scoping, no-store caching, and request throttling are enforced.
- The threat model is based on local source code and tests, not a production configuration review, penetration test, legal determination, or proof that the recorded Chama policy matches its constitution.
- Real lending should remain blocked until the Chama has supplied its thresholds, the responsible legal entities and applicable credit rules have been assessed, the human-review operation and DPIA questions are resolved, and actual off-App disbursement can be reconciled to the schedule.
